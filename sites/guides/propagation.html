Workspace, Hygiene & Permissions

Set up a clean propagation station

  • Tray or baking sheet to contain mess
  • Sharp knife or secateurs, rubbing alcohol, kitchen roll
  • Labels + pencil, small pots or cell inserts, clear bag or dome
  • Mix: 50:50 peat-free compost : perlite (or 60:30:10 compost : bark fibre : perlite)

Sterilise between plants: wipe tools with alcohol between plants to reduce rot and fungal spread.

Ethics & permissions

  • Propagate only from plants you own or have permission to take from
  • Some ornamentals are under Plant Breeders’ Rights
  • Never collect from the wild without explicit consent

Healthy donors give the best cuttings. Feed and water a few days before taking material.

Softwood Cuttings Spring–Early Summer

For salvias, pelargonium, coleus, basil, mint, thyme, oregano.

  1. Cut 8–10 cm tip below a node; strip lower leaves.
  2. Optional hormone dip; insert into airy mix; firm gently.
  3. Water; cover with clear dome/bag held off foliage.
  4. Bright, indirect light; 18–22 °C; vent daily. Root in 2–4 weeks.

Take several small cuttings rather than one chunky stem—faster strike, better branching.

Semi-ripe & Hardwood Cuttings

Semi-ripe late summer for rosemary, lavender, many salvias. Hardwood late autumn–winter for figs, currants, willow, dogwood.

  1. Semi-ripe: 8–12 cm tips; 40/40/20 mix; gentle bottom warmth.
  2. Hardwood: 15–20 cm leafless sticks; two nodes buried; cool gritty mix.
  3. Keep barely moist; ventilate; shelter from hard frost.

Most woody failures = cold + wet compost. Err on the dry side and ventilate.

Division

For chives, mint, oregano, thyme, culinary sage, hosta, daylily, grasses, strawberries (via runners).

  1. Water parent plant the day before.
  2. Tip out, tease or slice into sections with roots + shoots.
  3. Pot into fresh mix; trim top growth by one-third; shade 2–3 days.

Rejuvenate chives and sage by dividing every 2–3 years.

Layering

Zero-drama for trailing strawberries, thyme, oregano and arching salvias.

  1. Nick underside of a low stem at a node; pin into compost in a nearby pot.
  2. Keep moist; roots form in 2–4 weeks.
  3. Cut free and pot on.

Great for older salvias that sulk as cuttings.

Water Propagation

For basil, mint, coleus, pothos, monstera and many soft stems.

  1. 10 cm cutting; strip lower leaves; submerge nodes in water.
  2. Change water every 2–3 days.
  3. Pot up at 3–5 cm roots; shade lightly for 2–3 days.

Water roots are fragile—pot before they get long and feathery.

Grafting (Advanced)

Useful for tomatoes/cucurbits; seldom needed for salvias.

  1. Match stem widths (3–4 mm) of rootstock and scion.
  2. Cut both at 45°, align cambium, clip with silicone grafting clip.
  3. High humidity + shade 5–7 days, then wean.

Houseplants & Tropicals

  • Pothos, Monstera, Philodendron: stem cuttings in water; pot when roots 3–5 cm.
  • Spider plant: detach and pot pups when roots show.
  • Begonias: leaf/petiole cuttings; warm, humid spot.

Herbs & Edibles

  • Mint, basil, oregano, thyme: softwood or water-prop; bright light.
  • Chives, sage: divide clumps every 2–3 years.
  • Strawberries: layer runners into neighbour pots; cut after rooting.

Rooting Hormones & Natural Aids

  • Commercial powders/gels are reliable for woody salvias, rosemary and lavender.
  • Willow “tea” is mild—best as a soak for hardwood sticks.
  • Cinnamon = antifungal dust for cut surfaces (not a rooting stimulant).

Troubleshooting

Blackening stems

  • Too wet or too cold. Refresh cut; switch to drier airy mix.
  • Warm the base; add airflow; reduce dome time.

Leaves wilt, then crisp

  • Heat/sun stress. Move out of midday sun; mist lightly.
  • Reduce leaf area by a third on floppy salvias.

No roots after 4–6 weeks

  • Material too old/cold. Take fresher softwood or switch to semi-ripe timing.
  • Try a smaller cutting; refresh the mix.

Rooted but stall after potting

  • Pot too big or too rich. Step up gradually; feed half-strength.
  • Shade 2–3 days; keep just moist.

FAQs

Water-prop vs soil: which is better?
Water is fast and visible for basil, mint and many houseplants. Soil produces tougher roots for woody salvias, rosemary and lavender. If you water-prop salvias, pot early into a very airy mix.
Can I root flowering stems?
Pinch off flower buds. Rooting energy should go to new roots/leaves, not blooms.
Do I need heat mats?
Not essential, but gentle 18–22 °C base warmth raises success for semi-ripe cuttings.
Best first pot size?
Start small 7–9 cm; step up as roots fill. Oversized pots stay wet and promote rot.
