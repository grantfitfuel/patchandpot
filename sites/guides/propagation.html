Propagation: Cuttings, Division & Grafting

Small-space methods for salvias, herbs, edibles and ornamentals. Works on a windowsill, balcony or kitchen table.

Download Full Handbook (PDF)

Set up a tiny station

  • Tray or baking sheet to contain mess
  • Sharp knife or secateurs, alcohol wipes
  • Labels + pencil, small pots/cells, clear bag or dome
  • Mix: 50:50 peat-free compost : perlite (or 60:30:10 compost : bark fibre : perlite)

Sterilise between plants: wipe tools with alcohol to reduce rot and fungal spread.

Ethics & permissions

  • Propagate only from plants you own or have permission to take from
  • Some ornamentals carry Plant Breeders’ Rights
  • Don’t collect in the wild without explicit permission

Feed and water the donor a few days before taking material.

Softwood Cuttings Spring–Early Summer

Good for Salvia nemorosa, S. microphylla/greggii, basil, mint, thyme, sage, pelargonium, coleus.

  1. Take 8–10 cm non-flowering tip below a node; strip lower leaves.
  2. Optional hormone. Insert in 50:50 compost:perlite. Firm.
  3. Water; cover with clear dome/bag held off foliage.
  4. Bright, indirect light; 18–22 °C base; vent daily. Root in 2–4 wks.

Smaller salvias root faster and branch better.

Semi-ripe & Hardwood

Semi-ripe late summer for rosemary, lavender, many salvias. Hardwood winter for currants, willow, figs.

  1. Semi-ripe: 8–12 cm tips; 40/40/20 mix; gentle bottom warmth helps.
  2. Hardwood: 15–20 cm leafless sticks, pencil-thick; bury two nodes in gritty mix.
  3. Keep barely moist; shelter from hard frost; root across winter.

Most woody failures = cold + wet. Go drier, ventilate.

Division

Fast for clumps: chives, mint, oregano, thyme, culinary sage, daylily, hosta, grasses, strawberries (runners).

  1. Water parent plant the day before.
  2. Tease or slice into pieces with roots + shoots.
  3. Pot into fresh mix; trim top growth by one-third; shade 2–3 days.

Layering

Zero-drama for strawberries, thyme, oregano, woody salvias with arching stems.

  1. Nick underside at a node; pin to compost (paperclip).
  2. Keep moist; roots in 2–4 weeks.
  3. Cut free and pot on.

Water Propagation

Great for basil, mint, coleus and soft salvias.

  1. 10 cm tip below a node; strip lower leaves.
  2. Jar of water covering nodes; change every 2–3 days.
  3. Pot at 3–5 cm roots; light shade 2–3 days.

Water-roots are fragile; don’t let them get long and feathery.

Grafting (optional)

Common for tomatoes/cucurbits for disease resistance and cool-summer vigour.

  1. Raise rootstock + scion to ~3–4 mm stems.
  2. Cut both at 45°, align cambium, clip.
  3. High humidity + shade 5–7 days; then wean to light.

Humidity, Light & Airflow

  • Dome/bag for humidity; vent daily
  • Bright, indirect light; avoid midday scorch
  • Gentle fan or cracked window strengthens stems

If leaves sweat inside the dome, raise it with pegs/chopsticks.

Potting-On & Hardening

  • Pot when a gentle tug meets resistance or roots reach 3–4 cm
  • Feed half-strength after 7–10 days
  • Harden off 7–10 days before living outdoors full-time

Newly rooted salvias may flop for a day after potting—shade and they perk up.

Rooting Hormones & Natural Aids

  • Commercial powders/gels: reliable for woody salvias and rosemary
  • Willow water: mild; best as a soak for hardwoods
  • Cinnamon: antifungal dust for cuts (not a rooting stimulant)

Blackening stems

  • Too wet or cold → refresh cut, drier airy mix
  • Warm base, add airflow, reduce dome time

No roots after 4–6 weeks

  • Material too old/cold → take fresher softwood or go semi-ripe timing
  • Try a smaller cutting; refresh the mix

FAQs

Water-prop vs soil: which is better?
Water is fast and visible for basil and mint. Soil produces tougher roots for woody salvias and rosemary. If you water-prop salvias, pot early and use a very airy mix.
Can I root flowering stems?
Pinch off flower buds. Rooting energy should go to new roots and leaves, not blooms.
Do I need heat mats?
Not essential, but gentle 18–22 °C base warmth increases success for semi-ripe cuttings.
How big should the first pot be?
Start small 7–9 cm. Step up as roots fill. Oversized pots stay wet and promote rot.
