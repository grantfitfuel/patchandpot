Why Propagate?

Propagation saves money, reduces waste, and gives you healthy, vigorous plants that adapt well to your space. Softwood cuttings can become mature plants in weeks, while division and layering keep older plants productive.

Propagation Methods

Softwood Cuttings

Perfect for salvias, basil, mint, oregano, thyme, pelargoniums.

  1. Take 8–10 cm tip cutting below a node, strip lower leaves.
  2. Dip in rooting powder (optional) and insert into mix.
  3. Water, cover with dome or bag, ventilate daily.

Multiple small cuttings root faster than one chunky stem.

Semi-ripe & Hardwood

Good for rosemary, lavender, woody salvias, currants, figs.

  1. Take 8–12 cm semi-ripe tip or hardwood stick.
  2. Insert into airy mix. Shelter over winter for hardwood.

Cold + wet = failure. Keep mix just damp and ventilate.

Division

Best for chives, thyme, oregano, strawberries, hostas.

  1. Water day before. Tip out, tease or slice clump into sections.
  2. Replant into fresh mix. Trim top growth by one-third.

Layering

Easy, no-cut propagation for strawberries, thyme, oregano, salvias.

  1. Nick underside of stem, peg into neighbour pot.
  2. Keep moist. Roots in 2–4 weeks. Cut free & pot on.

Water Propagation

Mint, basil, salvias — simple on a windowsill.

  1. Strip lower leaves, place in water jar covering nodes.
  2. Change water every 2–3 days. Pot up at 3–5 cm roots.

Water roots are fragile. Don’t let them get too long.

Grafting (Advanced)

Used mainly for tomatoes and cucurbits.

  1. Match stem widths, cut both at 45°, align cambium.
  2. Clip, high humidity 5–7 days, then wean to normal light.

Troubleshooting

Black stems

  • Too wet or too cold — refresh cutting & mix.
  • Warm base + airflow helps.

Wilt & crisp leaves

  • Heat stress — move out of midday sun.

No roots after 6 wks

  • Material too old or cold. Take fresher cuttings.

Stall after potting

  • Pot too big or too rich. Step up gradually.
